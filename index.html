One decision, written down for whoever comes next

An ADR documents a single significant choice: the context that made it necessary, the decision itself, and its consequences. Together, a project's ADRs form its decision log.

Context

The constraints that make the decision necessary, stated neutrally.

Decision

The choice made, as an active commitment: We will ...

Consequences

Positive and negative results, both listed.

Compliance optional

Concrete, checkable rules that follow from the decision.

When an ADR is worth writing

Write one for decisions about structure, non-functional requirements, dependencies, interfaces and construction techniques. Skip it for routine detail.

Write an ADR for

Structure

Architectural patterns, such as microservices over a monolith.

Non-functional requirements

Security, high availability, fault tolerance.

Dependencies

Coupling between components or services.

Interfaces

APIs and published contracts.

Construction techniques

Libraries, frameworks, tools, and processes.

Skip the ADR for
  • Routine implementation detail
  • A single function's internals
  • Anything easily reversible with no cross-team impact

A short, strict lifecycle

A new ADR is Proposed, reviewed, then Accepted or Rejected. Both are immutable: a change of direction becomes a new ADR that supersedes the old one.

Proposed

The owner drafts the ADR and opens it for review.

Review

The team reads it (10-15 minutes), then discusses.

Accepted and Rejected are immutable.A change of direction becomes a new ADR.
Changes needed

Stays Proposed

The owner actions each comment, then reschedules the review.

back to Review
Team agrees

Accepted

The owner adds date, version, and stakeholders.

immutable
A new ADR is accepted

Superseded

Marked "Superseded by ADR-NNNN" and linked to the new ADR. Never deleted.

Team declines

Rejected

The owner records the reason, so it is not proposed again.

immutable

TitleADR-0007: We use an event bus between services
StatusAccepted
Date2026-01-15
ContextServices call each other directly, so one slow service can delay the others.
DecisionWe will publish domain events to a shared bus instead of calling services directly.
Consequences
  • Positive:Services fail independently.
  • Negative:We now operate and monitor the bus.
ComplianceNew services publish events through the bus, not through direct calls.
