ADR lifecycleaws-adr
Proposed
The owner drafts the ADR and opens it for review.
Review
The team reads it (10-15 minutes), then discusses.
Stays Proposed
The owner actions each comment, then reschedules the review.
Accepted
The owner adds date, version, and stakeholders.
immutable
Rejected
The owner records the reason, so it is not proposed again.
immutable
Superseded
Marked "Superseded by ADR-NNNN" and linked to the new ADR. Never deleted.
The eight sectionsaws-adr
1TitleNames the decision, not the problem.
2StatusProposed, Accepted, Rejected, or Superseded by ADR-NNNN.
3DateThe date the status last changed.
4ContextThe constraints that make the decision necessary, stated neutrally.
5DecisionThe choice made, as an active commitment: "We will ..."
6ConsequencesThe results of the decision, split into two lists.Positive: Negative:
7ComplianceConcrete, checkable rules that follow from the decision.optional
8NotesAuthor, version, and a changelog.optional
When to write an ADRaws-adr
Write an ADR for
Structure
Architectural patterns, such as microservices over a monolith.
Non-functional requirements
Security, high availability, fault tolerance.
Dependencies
Coupling between components or services.
Interfaces
APIs and published contracts.
Construction techniques
Libraries, frameworks, tools, and processes.
Skip the ADR for
  • Routine implementation detail
  • A single function's internals
  • Anything easily reversible with no cross-team impact
